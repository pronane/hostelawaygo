# Who else is in this market, and what to take from them

Checked October–November 2026. Five groups compete for the same ten minutes in a
hostel: the bed marketplaces, the "meet people" apps, the city-guide apps, the
hostel software, and the attraction resellers. None of them is trying to be what
Away is, and that is the opening — but each one has already taught the traveller
to expect something.

The question this answers: *what would make someone choose this over the app
they already have open?*

---

## 1. The map

| Group | Who | What they sell | What they are good at | Where they are weak for a hostel traveller |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bed marketplaces** | Hostelworld, Hostelz, Booking.com, Hostelsclub, HI | Beds, 15–17.7% commission | Inventory, reviews, trust, an app the traveller already has | Their business is the booking, not the Tuesday night in the building |
| **Meet-people apps** | Nomadtable, TripBFF, Travello, Backpackr, GAFFL, Hellohood, Timeleft, Bumble BFF, Meetup, Couchsurfing | Connection, sometimes a paid pass or a paid dinner | Solves loneliness directly; strong app-store presence | Nothing to do with the hostel's own programme, no supply, no local knowledge |
| **City guides** | Time Out, Fever, Resident Advisor, getyourguide, hostelz "social score" | Discovery and tickets | Editorial depth, SEO, ticketing | Written for residents or tourists, not for "I have two nights and no plan" |
| **Hostel software** | Cloudbeds, Sirvoy, Frontdesk, BananaDesk, Mews | PMS, bookings, channel manager | Runs the property | Does not talk to the guest after check-in; nothing to do with the neighbourhood |
| **Attraction resellers** | Viator (8% affiliate), The Host Co (7%), Localbird, Canary | Tours, upsells | Distribution and payouts at scale | Commission on the guest's fun; the hostel is a reseller of somebody else's product |

### The numbers that matter

- **Hostelworld:** standard commission **15%**, effective **17.7%** in H1 2026
  because of Elevate (paying for placement). ~14,000 hostels. A 60-bed hostel at
  75% occupancy pays roughly **€2,000–2,400 a month** in commission.
- **Hostelworld Social Pass** (launched Nov 2025): **€4.99/week, €9.99/month,
  €19.99/3 months, €59.99/year**, one-off, no renewal. It is the social layer
  sold *without a booking* — the clearest signal that "the social thing" is
  worth money on its own. About a third of buyers were new to the platform.
- **Hostelz:** 22,500+ properties, price comparison across Hostelworld /
  Booking / hostelsclub / direct, plus a "Social Score" filter. No social
  features of its own — it is a shop window for other people's beds.
- **Nomadtable:** ~2M users, 20,000+ App Store ratings (4.7), free with a Plus
  tier. The largest app built *only* for travellers meeting travellers.
- **Viator** pays partners **8%** on experience bookings; **The Host Co**
  charges 7% on the operator's own sales; **Canary** deliberately charges 0% to
  win the upsell layer. The market has already decided that commission on top of
  a bed is unpopular with hosts.
- **Timeleft:** paid dinners, per booking. **Couchsurfing:** paid membership
  (~$2.39/month equivalent). Both prove travellers pay small amounts for
  company — but they pay for a *stranger-matching service*, not for a hostel.

## 2. What each of them gets right, and what we should take

### Take: Hostelworld's Social Pass pricing
They sell one week for **€4.99** and one month for **€9.99** as a **one-off
purchase, no auto-renewal**. That is a much easier yes than a subscription, and
it validates the idea that a traveller will pay a hostel-night's worth of money
for access to people and plans.

*For Away:* the free tier is the product and it should stay free for anyone
staying in a partner hostel. But a **Traveller Pass** for someone not staying in
a hostel (`€4.99 a week`, nothing recurring) is an obvious second revenue line
once a city is live: the same feed, the same groups, the same safe-night links.
It also converts the "I'm in an Airbnb" traveller, who is currently locked out
of the whole product.

### Take: Hostelworld's "Linkups" and the pre-arrival chat window
Their chats open **seven days before check-in**. That is the lesson: the moment
of maximum intent is *before* arrival, not at the desk. Away's arrivals digest
does some of this by email; the app should do it in-product — "you arrive
Thursday, 3 things are on that night, 2 people in your dorm are going".

### Take: Hostelz's "Social Score" filter
A single comparable number for *how social is this hostel* is the thing every
solo traveller wants and no hostel can self-report honestly. Away can compute it
from behaviour it already has: how many guests joined something, how many nights
the hostel posted, how many groups formed. **"This hostel had 4 nights on and 12
guests out last week"** is a much stronger claim than a star rating, and it is
unfakeable in a way reviews are not.

### Take: Nomadtable's tiny unit of content
Their whole product is "someone nearby posted a small plan; join it". Away's
events are heavier (a hostel posts a night). **A one-line, no-host plan** —
"sunset at the miradouro, 19:00, 3 going" — posted by any traveller, is the
missing middle: cheaper than an event, more concrete than a chat message. It is
also the natural growth loop when a city has one hostel and no programme.

### Take: Viator / The Host Co's zero-friction upsell
Neither asks the hostel to build inventory; they surface somebody else's and
take a cut (or none, in Canary's case). Away should do the **opposite of the
commission**: surface the city's ticketed events (already ingested) with a
booking link, take **no cut**, and use it purely to make the feed complete.
Distribution is the moat; a few percent of a walking tour is not.

### Don't take: Elevate
Paying for placement is exactly the incentive that turned a 15% commission into
17.7%. Away's pitch is "no commission, no placement fee, €19 flat". If a
"featured" slot ever goes on sale, the pitch dies with it.

### Don't take: the app-store app
Every incumbent has a native app; none of them can be opened by pointing a
camera at a poster. The QR code *is* the install, and 150 MB of download is a
conversion tax that a hostel guest at 19:00 will not pay.

### Don't take: stranger-matching, profiles, swiping
Timeleft, BFF, TripBFF and Backpackr all sell a 1:1 match. It is the highest
churn, highest-abuse, highest-support product in travel, and it does not make a
hostel's bar busier. Group links and "who else is arriving" get the same
outcome — people not being alone — with none of the moderation.

## 3. Where Away is genuinely different (and should say so)

| | Hostelworld | Nomadtable | Away |
|---|---|---|---|
| Where the supply comes from | Global inventory | Users posting plans | **The hostel's own programme, posted at the desk in a minute** |
| Price to the hostel | 15–17.7% of every bed | — | **€0 to be listed, €19/mo for the screen and the receipt** |
| How a traveller arrives | App store | App store | **QR on the reception wall / dorm door** |
| Who a group is | A chat room | A trip | **A link the hostel can see coming ("2 in a group")** |
| What the hostel gets back | A booking | Nothing | **Scans → joins → beds, and a weekly receipt** |
| Works without a booking? | Only with a paid pass | Yes | **Yes — the feed is public** |

The one-sentence version: *Hostelworld sells the bed and hopes you socialise;
Nomadtable sells the socialising and has no beds; Away is the hostel's own
programme, with the beds attached.*

## 4. What to build because of this

Ranked by "does it answer a competitor the traveller already uses".

1. **A Social Score, computed from behaviour** *(S)* — "4 nights on, 12 guests
   out last week" on the hostel page and in Explore. It is Hostelz's best filter
   with data only a hostel's own operations can produce.
2. **One-line traveller plans** *(M)* — "sunset at the miradouro, 19:00" posted
   by anyone, no hostel required. This is Nomadtable's whole product, but with
   the hostel as the distribution and the group link already built.
3. **Open chats a week before arrival** *(S)* — Hostelworld's window, applied to
   the arrivals already in the database.
4. **A Traveller Pass for people not in a hostel** *(M)* — €4.99 a week, one-off,
   exactly the Social Pass shape. It is the only way to reach the Airbnb guest,
   and it proves the social layer is worth money on its own.
5. **No-commission city events with a booking link** *(S)* — the anti-Viator:
   complete the feed, take nothing, keep the trust.
6. **Group rates and split payment** *(M)* — the hostel-facing half of groups,
   where Hostelworld's commission bites hardest (a 9-bed group is €180 of bed
   value and €32 of commission).

Things explicitly not on this list, with reasons: paid placement (kills the
pitch), a native app (the QR is the install), 1:1 matching (moderation for
nothing), scraping social networks (loses the partner), and any cut of a bed.
